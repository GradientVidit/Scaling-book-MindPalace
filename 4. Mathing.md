
- **Dot product:** `2P FLOPs`
- **Matmul:** `A[N,P] × B[P,M] → C[N,M]` = `2NPM FLOPs`
- Matmul compute grows as **`O(N³)`**, while data movement grows as **`O(N²)`** → larger matmuls are easier to make compute-bound.

### Training FLOPs

For a matmul with `P×M` parameters:
- Forward = `2 × params` FLOPs
- Backward = `4 × params` FLOPs
- **Training ≈ `6 × params × tokens` FLOPs**

> **Dense Transformer training FLOPs ≈ `6 × model parameters × training tokens`**

### Transformer Dimensions

- `B` = batch size
- `S` = sequence length
- `D` = model dimension
- `F` = FFN dimension
- `N` = number of Q heads
- `K` = number of KV heads
- `H` = head dimension
- `L` = number of Transformer layers

### MLP
- Parameters/layer ≈ **`3DF`**
- Training FLOPs/layer ≈ **`18BSDF`**

### Attention Projections
- Parameters/layer ≈ **`2D(N+K)H`**
- Training FLOPs/layer ≈ **`12BSD(N+K)H`**

### Attention — QKᵀ + AV
- Training FLOPs/layer ≈ **`12BS²NH`**
- Cost is **quadratic in sequence length `S`**.

For typical:
- `F = 4D`
- `D = NH`
- `N = K`

Attention dot-product FLOPs become dominant around:

> **`S > 8D`**

Example: `D = 8192` → **~64K context**.

### Key Takeaways

1. **MLP dominates** Transformer parameters and usually FLOPs.
    
2. **`6 × params × tokens`** is the standard dense-Transformer training FLOP estimate.
    
3. Attention's **`S²`** cost becomes important at very long context.
    
4. **MoE:** parameters scale with total experts `E`, while compute scales with activated experts `k`, typically `k ≪ E`.
    
5. MoE requires **two AllToAlls**: dispatch tokens to experts and gather them back.
    
6. **Gradient checkpointing/rematerialization** trades memory for additional compute.
    
7. **KV cache per sequence:** `2 × S × L × K × H` elements.
    - `S` = sequence length
    - `L` = number of layers
    - `K` = number of KV heads
    - `H` = head dimension
    - `2` = K + V
        
8. **GQA/MQA reduce KV-cache memory** by reducing `K` (number of KV heads).
